f' and f'' are stored as constants and enter Fc unchanged at every scattering angle, while f0 decays. This gives them a decay of their own, the same one for both, refined with the structure:

  f'(u) = f' R(u),   f''(u) = f'' R(u),   R(u) = 1 + c1u + c2u2 + ...

u is 1 − cos 2θ, which is what the physics asks for, or sin(θ)/λ with spy.disprad.setup basis=stol. These are not just different units. Since cos 2θ = 1 − 2λ2s2, a polynomial in the first is exactly a polynomial of the same degree in s2 — the even powers of s and only those — whereas powers of s span the odd ones too, and those are not polynomials in cos 2θ at all. Choosing cos 2θ restricts the model to the physical family rather than rewriting it, and it makes the correction turn on quadratically from forward scattering instead of linearly.

There is no constant term: scaling f' by a constant is the same as changing f', so it would be exactly degenerate. Both choices of u vanish at θ = 0, so R(0) = 1 and the tabulated values stand untouched at forward scattering, where they are by definition right.

The coefficients start at zero, where R is 1 and nothing has changed. They correlate strongly with U of the same atoms, and with f' and f'' themselves where those are refined too, so treat a large correction with suspicion: it is at least as likely to be absorbing model error as measuring dispersion. R is held above 0.1 over the measured resolution range, since f' and f'' changing sign is not something the model should be allowed to do quietly.

The dropdown here groups by element. One group per atom, values put in by hand, and holding one group while another refines are command line only:

  spy.disprad.setup atoms=Pd1 n=1
  spy.disprad.set group=Pd c=-1.8,2.0 fix=True
  spy.disprad.print_current

Values put there by hand stand until you change them; refined values are reused as a starting point only while they still match the model they came from. Twinned data is refused.
