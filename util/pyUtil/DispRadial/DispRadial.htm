A radial falloff shared by f' and f''.

f' and f'' are stored as constants and enter Fc unchanged at every scattering angle, while f0 decays. This gives them a decay of their own, the same one for both, refined with the structure:

  f'(u) = f' R(u),   f''(u) = f'' R(u),   R(u) = 1 + c1u + c2u2 + ...

u is 1 − cos 2θ by default, which is what the physics asks for, or sin(θ)/λ with basis=stol. These are not just different units. Since cos 2θ = 1 − 2λ2s2, a polynomial in the first is exactly a polynomial of the same degree in s2 — the even powers of s and only those — whereas powers of s span the odd ones too, and those are not polynomials in cos 2θ at all. Choosing cos 2θ restricts the model to the physical family rather than rewriting it, and it makes the correction turn on quadratically from forward scattering instead of linearly.

There is no constant term: scaling f' by a constant is the same as changing f', so it would be exactly degenerate. Both choices of u vanish at θ = 0, so R(0) = 1 and the tabulated values stand untouched at forward scattering, where they are by definition right.

There are no GUI controls yet. It is driven from the command line: 

  disprad.setup elements=Pd n=2
  disprad.setup atoms=Pd1 n=1 basis=stol
  disprad.print_current
  disprad.plot
  disprad.remove

Values of your own can be asserted rather than fitted. Held fixed the correction still multiplies f' and f'' — it shapes the model the R factors describe — it simply contributes no parameter to the least squares, and gets no uncertainty: 

  disprad.set group=Pd c=-1.8,2.0 fix=True
  disprad.set c=-1.8,2.0,-0.3,0.1        every coefficient, group-major
  disprad.fix                            hold whatever is there now
  disprad.refine                         let them go again

Values put there by hand stand until you change them; refined values are reused as a starting point only while they still match the model they came from. Note that fixing is all or nothing — there is no way yet to hold one group and refine another.

The coefficients start at zero, where R is 1 and nothing has changed. They correlate strongly with U of the same atoms, and with f' and f'' themselves where those are refined too, so treat a large correction with suspicion: it is at least as likely to be absorbing model error as measuring dispersion. R is held above 0.1 over the measured resolution range, since f' and f'' changing sign is not something the model should be allowed to do quietly.

disprad.plot draws f' and f'' against sin(θ)/λ over the measured resolution range: a flat line for each tabulated value and a curve for what the refinement made of it. How far a curve departs from its line is the size of the correction, and the further it goes the more likely it is to be absorbing model error rather than measuring dispersion.
